Raw manure and compost

Module 02, Topic 3 of 5. Estimated time: 12 minutes.

What this topic is for

Apply safe handling practices for raw manure and compost, including application intervals and storage.

Raw manure

Raw manure is a valuable fertilizer and a major source of E. coli and Salmonella. The safest practice is to apply it long before harvest and never in a way that touches the edible part of the crop.

As an example, organic certification programs use an interval of 120 days between application and harvest when the edible part touches the soil, and 90 days when it does not. Use the interval that applies to your certification or market.

Treated compost

Compost lowers risk only when it is treated with a documented time and temperature process. A pile that never got hot, or a process with no records, should be handled like raw manure.

Storage

  • Store piles away from fields, water sources, and packing areas.
  • Cover piles or place them where runoff cannot reach produce.
  • Clean equipment used for manure before it touches anything near the crop.

Knowledge check

1. A neighbor gives you compost with no records of how hot it got. How should you treat it?

  1. As treated compost, ready to use
  2. As raw manure, with the same intervals and care
  3. As safe for leafy greens if you water it in
Show the answer to question 1

B. Without documented time and temperature, you cannot show the pathogens were killed, so handle it like raw manure.

Check yourself

Where do you store manure or compost now, and could runoff from it reach a field or water source?
