Reading a water test

Module 02, Topic 2 of 5. Estimated time: 12 minutes.

What this topic is for

Read a generic E. coli water test result and decide what to do next.

What the lab is measuring

Labs test agricultural water for generic E. coli. Most strains are harmless, but their presence signals fecal contamination, which means harmful pathogens could be present too. That is why generic E. coli is called an indicator.

Reading the result

  • Results are usually reported as colony forming units (CFU) or most probable number (MPN) per 100 milliliters.
  • One result is a snapshot. Several samples across the season show a pattern.
  • Keep every lab report. It is part of your records in Module 04.
Check the current rule

Federal requirements for pre-harvest agricultural water changed in recent years and now center on an annual assessment of your water system. Confirm what applies to your farm with your state produce safety program before you set a testing schedule.

Knowledge check

1. Your pond water test shows generic E. coli well above your past results, two weeks before harvest. What is the best next step?

  1. Keep irrigating and test again next season
  2. Stop using the pond on the crop, look for the cause, and retest
  3. Wash the produce longer after harvest
Show the answer to question 1

B. A spike signals new contamination. Stop the exposure, find the source, and confirm with a retest. Longer washing does not remove attached bacteria.

Check yourself

When did you last test each water source on your farm, and where is the lab report?
