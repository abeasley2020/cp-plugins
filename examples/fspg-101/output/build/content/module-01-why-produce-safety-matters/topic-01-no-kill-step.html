Purdue University Online FSPG 101 Module 01: Why produce safety matters

Why produce eaten raw is different

Module 01, Topic 1 of 4. Estimated time: 10 minutes.

What this topic is for

Explain why produce eaten raw carries more risk than food that is cooked, so every practice in this course has a clear reason behind it.

No kill step between the field and the plate

Most food safety systems rely on a kill step: cooking, pasteurizing, or another treatment that destroys harmful microbes before anyone eats the food. A salad, a tomato, or a bunch of strawberries usually has none. Whatever reaches the produce in the field can reach the person who eats it.

That is why produce safety is about prevention. The goal is to keep contamination off the crop in the first place, because there is no reliable way to remove it later.

Why this matters

One outbreak traced to a small farm can end its wholesale accounts and its farmers market sales in the same season. Prevention is cheaper than recovery.

Washing is not a fix

Growers often assume a good rinse will take care of it. Washing removes soil, but it does not reliably remove bacteria that have attached to the surface or entered through damage. Poorly managed wash water can spread contamination from one item to an entire batch.

Optional depth: why can wash water make things worse?

When many items share the same water, microbes from one contaminated item move into the water and onto the rest. Changing water often and keeping sanitizer at label strength reduces that risk, which Module 04 covers in detail.

Retrieval practice

Commit to an answer before opening each card.

What is a kill step?

A treatment, such as cooking or pasteurizing, that destroys harmful microbes before food is eaten. Most fresh produce has none.

Why is washing alone not enough?

Washing removes soil but not attached bacteria, and shared wash water can spread contamination across a batch.
