Purdue University Online FSPG 101 Module 01: Why produce safety matters

Five routes of contamination

Module 01, Topic 3 of 4. Estimated time: 12 minutes.

What this topic is for

Trace the five routes contamination travels on a farm so you can spot them on your own operation.

The five routes

Produce safety training groups farm hazards into five routes. Every practice in the rest of this course controls one or more of them.

  1. Water used for irrigation, sprays, washing, and cleaning.
  2. Soil and soil amendments, especially raw manure and compost that was not fully treated.
  3. Animals, both wild and domestic, that enter growing areas.
  4. Workers, through their hands, their health, and their habits.
  5. Equipment, tools, and surfaces, including harvest bins and packing tables.
Route 1: water

Water touches the crop many times. Surface water such as ponds and streams carries the most risk because animals and runoff can reach it. Module 02 covers water in depth.

Route 2: soil and amendments

Raw manure can carry E. coli and Salmonella. Compost is safer only when it is treated with documented time and temperature.

Route 3: animals

Deer, birds, and pets leave feces in fields. You cannot fence out every animal, so the practice is to look, assess, and avoid harvesting near contamination.

Route 4: workers

Hands carry microbes from restrooms, soil, and animals to the crop. Sick workers are the main source of viruses.

Route 5: equipment and surfaces

Bins, knives, and tables move microbes from one batch to the next if they are not cleaned and sanitized.

Prevention beats correction

Controls applied before harvest do more than anything you can do afterward.

Knowledge check

1. A grower stacks harvest bins on the ground next to the compost pile. Which two routes does this open?

  1. Water and workers
  2. Soil amendments and equipment
  3. Animals and water
Show the answer to question 1

B. The compost pile is a soil amendment route, and the bins are equipment that will carry what they touch into the harvest.

Check yourself

Name one route that is present on your farm today and one change you could make this week to control it.
