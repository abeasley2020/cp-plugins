Purdue University Online FSPG 101 Module 01: Why produce safety matters

The pathogens that matter on a farm

Module 01, Topic 2 of 4. Estimated time: 12 minutes.

What this topic is for

Recognize the bacteria and viruses most often linked to produce outbreaks and know where each one comes from, so you can tell which practices protect against which hazard.

Two kinds of hazards, two kinds of sources

The microbes that cause produce outbreaks fall into two groups. Bacteria mostly come from animal feces and the environment. Viruses mostly come from people, especially workers who handle produce while sick.

Pathogens linked to fresh produce and where they come from
PathogenTypeUsual source on a farm
E. coli O157:H7BacteriumFeces from cattle and other animals, carried by water, manure, or wildlife
SalmonellaBacteriumFeces from animals including birds and reptiles; contaminated water
Listeria monocytogenesBacteriumSoil and damp packing areas; it can grow in cool, wet places
NorovirusVirusSick workers, through hands and poor handwashing
Hepatitis AVirusInfected workers, sometimes before they feel ill
Key idea

Match the control to the source. Water and manure practices target bacteria. Worker health and handwashing practices target viruses.

Retrieval practice

Commit to an answer before opening each card.

Which pathogen can grow in a cool, damp packing shed?

Listeria monocytogenes. It survives and grows in cool, wet environments, which is why packing area sanitation matters.

Where do norovirus and hepatitis A usually come from on a farm?

People. Sick or infected workers transfer them through their hands.

Knowledge check

1. A worker had vomiting last night and feels better this morning. Which hazard is the main concern if they harvest lettuce today?

  1. E. coli O157:H7 from soil
  2. Norovirus from the worker
  3. Listeria from the packing shed
Show the answer to question 1

B. Norovirus spreads from people and stays infectious after symptoms end. The other two come from the environment, not from this worker.

Check yourself

Which of these sources is present on your farm right now: livestock, wildlife, a pond, a packing shed, or seasonal workers?
