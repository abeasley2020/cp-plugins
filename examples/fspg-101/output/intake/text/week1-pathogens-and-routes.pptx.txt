[slide 1]
Why produce safety matters
Fresh produce is often eaten raw
No kill step between the field and the plate
Outbreaks hurt small farms the most
[notes] Tell the story of the cantaloupe outbreak and what it did to the grower. Stress that most contamination happens before harvest.
[slide 2]
The pathogens we worry about
E. coli O157:H7
Salmonella
Listeria monocytogenes
Norovirus and Hepatitis A from people
[notes] Bacteria come from animal feces; viruses mostly from sick workers. Listeria lives in packing environments.
[slide 3]
Five routes of contamination
Water
Soil and soil amendments
Animals, wild and domestic
Workers' hands and health
Equipment, tools, and surfaces
[notes] Use the farm walk diagram. Ask learners to name one route on their own farm.
[slide 4]
Prevention beats correction
You cannot wash contamination out of produce
Washing can spread it
Focus on controls before harvest
[notes] Wash water can cross-contaminate a whole batch if it is not managed.
