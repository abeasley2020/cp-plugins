# Course map: Food Safety for Small-Scale Produce Growers

- Code: FSPG 101
- College: College of Agriculture (sample course for testing)
- Audience: Small farm owners, market garden managers, and extension volunteers; no science background assumed
- Delivery: Online, self-paced, on-demand (converted from a live cohort syllabus)
- Format: module
- Total minutes: 232
- Completion time per page (seconds): 45
- Description: Learners identify food safety hazards on a produce farm and put practical controls in place, finishing with a one-page farm food safety plan.

## Course outcomes

- CLO1 (Understand): Explain how pathogens reach fresh produce and why produce eaten raw carries higher risk.
- CLO2 (Evaluate): Assess agricultural water sources and choose an appropriate testing and treatment approach.
- CLO3 (Apply): Apply safe handling practices for raw manure and compost on a small farm.
- CLO4 (Create): Design worker hygiene, harvest, and cleaning practices that reduce contamination.
- CLO5 (Create): Create a written farm food safety plan with records an inspector would accept.

## Module 01: Why produce safety matters

- Minutes: 54
- Build: yes
- Summary: Learners see why produce eaten raw carries risk, meet the pathogens that matter on a farm, and trace the five routes contamination travels.

### Objectives

- 1.1 (Understand; supports CLO1): Explain why fresh produce has no kill step between the field and the plate.
- 1.2 (Remember; supports CLO1): Identify the bacteria and viruses most often linked to produce outbreaks and where each comes from.
- 1.3 (Analyze; supports CLO1, CLO5): Trace the five routes of contamination on your own farm.

## Module 02: Water, soil amendments, and animals

- Minutes: 69
- Build: yes
- Summary: Learners rank water sources by risk, read a water test result, handle manure and compost safely, and decide what to do when animals get into a field.

### Objectives

- 2.1 (Evaluate; supports CLO2): Rank agricultural water sources by contamination risk and justify the ranking.
- 2.2 (Apply; supports CLO2, CLO5): Interpret a generic E. coli water test result and decide on a next step.
- 2.3 (Apply; supports CLO3): Apply raw manure and compost handling practices, including application intervals and storage.
- 2.4 (Evaluate; supports CLO3): Decide whether produce near animal intrusion can be harvested.

## Conversion notes

- The syllabus is written for a live cohort. The Week 3 Zoom discussion on handwashing stations becomes a self-check with a model answer (Activity 3). Lost: peer feedback on each station. Faculty should confirm the self-check meets the objective.
- Peer review of the final plan on the discussion board is replaced by a rubric and a worked example. Faculty should decide whether a graded instructor review is still offered.
- Week numbers and due dates were removed from module titles so the course can run on demand.
