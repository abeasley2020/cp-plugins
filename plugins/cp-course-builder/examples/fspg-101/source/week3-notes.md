# FSPG 101 week 3 notes (fictional example course)

This is a made-up source file so the example lesson can show how every claim
traces to a source line. It is not faculty material.

Slide 4. Hands are the most common route for moving pathogens from a worker
to ready-to-eat food. A sink that is hard to reach does not get used.

Slide 5. A complete handwash: wet with warm running water, apply soap, scrub
all surfaces for at least 10 to 15 seconds (backs of hands, between fingers,
under nails), rinse, dry with a single-use towel. The whole wash takes about
20 seconds.

Slide 6. Wash when entering the production area, after the restroom, after
touching the face or hair, after handling raw product or trash, and when
changing tasks. Gloves do not replace washing; wash before putting gloves on.

Slide 7. Hand sanitizer is used after washing, not instead of it. It works
poorly on wet, greasy or soiled hands.

Slide 8. Example: a line lead at a sandwich plant sees a worker go from the
trash bin straight back to the assembly line wearing the same gloves. The
lead has to decide whether to stop the line. A recall caused by a hand-borne
pathogen costs far more than a short stop.
